Abstract

The objective of this study is to evaluate the influence of internal control systems and the interactive use of the budget on organizational commitment, as mediated by managerial performance. A survey was carried out using Brazilian textile industry companies as a population, and the sample consists of 133 respondents to the questionnaire. Structural equation modeling was used to test the hypotheses. The results showed that internal control systems and/or the interactive use of the budget influence managerial performance, as well as influencing organizational commitment. The managerial performance variable had a total mediating effect on the relationship between the use of control systems and organizational commitment, indicating that internal control systems and the interactive use of the budget affect organizational commitment only when they are reflected in the managers’ performance. Based on the results, we concluded that internal control systems and the interactive use of the budget had influence on managerial performance and on the organizational commitment in the surveyed companies. These results advance the literature as they reveal that internal control systems and the interactive use of the budget are antecedent variables of managerial performance, leading to outcomes at work that can contribute to increasing organizational commitment.

Keywords

internal control systems, interactive use, budgetary control system, managerial performance, organizational commitment

1. Introduction

Budgetary planning and control can be an asset in the development and performance of the organization (Frezatti, Relvas, Nascimento, Junqueira & Bido, 2010). The budget “requires managers to make a great effort in detailing, involvement, and specification so that decisions are operationalized, which can be summarized in strategic planning” (Frezatti, Rocha, Nascimento & Junqueira, 2009, p. 60). In turn, budgetary control “corresponds to the closing of the managerial process and takes place not only before but during and after the activities occur” (Frezatti et al., 2009, p. 61).

The concept of control, described in Simons’ framework (1995), is closely linked to internal control systems and budgets. The structure proposed by the author, with four levers of control (LOCs) or systems (belief systems, boundary systems, interactive control systems, and diagnostic control systems), can help managers in internal control and budget control. Accordingly, the managers use numerical comparisons, for example, budgeted versus realized, or increases or decreases in expenses, among others, in order to periodically check for possible problems. In this study, Simons’ control model (1995) was used as a theoretical reference framework.

Control and budgetary effects have been researched, based on psychology precepts, on a variety of behaviors, as they recognize that individuals’ responses to their social environments are complex (Covaleski, Evans III, Luft & Shields, 2007). These potentially conflicting behaviors were investigated, taking into account different manifestations, such as motivation, stress, satisfaction, commitment, relationships with superiors, and managerial performance. One of the interests of this study is organizational commitment, characterized by a strong belief in and acceptance of the organization’s goals and values, a willingness to exert considerable effort on behalf of the organization, and a strong desire to maintain membership in it (Mowday, Steers & Porter, 1979). Managerial performance, in turn, is defined by Oh and Berry (2009) as managerial behaviors which are believed to be ideal for identifying, assimilating, and utilizing resources to support the organizational unit the manager is responsible for.

Nouri and Parker (1998) explored the links between budgetary participation and managerial performance. As limitations of the study, they pointed out several possibilities, among them: (i) organizational commitment leads to performance (causal direction generally assumed in the literature); (ii) performance leads to organizational commitment; and (iii) work performance and organizational commitment have a reciprocal relationship. This study follows the second possibility, in which performance leads to organizational commitment. A variety of approaches is found in the literature on this matter. Chong and Chong (2002) and Wentzel (2002) highlighted the relevance of studies on participative budgeting and its relationship with the commitment to budgetary goals. Young (1985), Chenhall (1986), Chow, Cooper and Waller (1988) and Shields and Shields (1998) prioritized approaches to participative budgeting. Argyris (1952) and Becker and Green (1962) focused on budget and individuals.

Covaleski et al. (2007) classify research that deals with the effects of budgetary practices in three stages. The first two discuss the different forms of the causal model for the independent variables - additive effects versus interactive effects. The third stage focuses on the relationships between the dependent variables of the models used in the first two phases - mental states, behavior and managerial performance. This approach verifies “how mental states mediate the effects of the budgetary practices on managerial behavior and performance” (Covaleski et al., 2007, p. 22).

However, other variables can influence one another and have effects on organizational commitment. For example, there is little evidence in research on managerial performance as a mediating variable influencing organizational commitment, even less when analyzed based on the levers proposed by Simons (1995), as in internal control systems and interactive use. As suggested by Mucci, Frezatti and Dieng (2016), future research could investigate the functions of the budget, the perceived importance of the instrument to the management, and decision making, or even relate these variables to the ways a budget could be used (diagnostic, interactive, coercive, or enabling).

Pondering these perspectives, there is the following research question: What is the influence of internal control systems and the interactive use of a budget on organizational commitment mediated by managerial performance? The objective of this study is to assess the influence of internal control systems and the interactive use of the budget on organizational commitment as mediated by managerial performance of companies in the Brazilian textile industry. It is assumed that this economic sector is imbued with innovative features of products and processes, with a view to meeting market demands (Rangel, Silva & Costa, 2010). This would imply the simultaneous use of internal control systems and interactive budgetary control systems, that is, concern with meeting pre-established goals and with flexibility to foster learning in the organization.

According to Rangel et al. (2010), the textile sector is divided into two types of markets. The first regards the commodity markets, with standardized products, which are destined to the population with less purchasing power. In this type of market, the strategic change is low, as competition is basically achieved through cost reductions and product prices. The second regards the segmented market, with differentiated products, aimed at the population with greater purchasing power. In this type of market, strategic change is high, as consumer demands are higher, they seek style and something unique or differentiated, design, and fashion, requiring companies to respond quickly to changes in fashion and consumer demands (Rangel et al., 2010). From this perspective, it is relevant to research the effects of using internal control systems and budgetary control systems in the textile industry.

The control levers proposed by Simons (1995) were used with a focus on budgets in the study conducted by Chong and Mahama (2014), to verify the impacts of interactive and diagnostic uses of the budget on the effectiveness of work teams in North American companies. This paper seeks to expand the literature on internal control systems and budgets, paying attention to control systems and to the managers’ organizational commitment. The scope of the research also considered the performance of the managers involved in this process, as a mediating variable, having effects on the relationship between internal control systems and the interactive use of the budgetary system and organizational commitment. Our aim is to contribute to understanding deficiencies related to the deeper aspects of organizational commitment, which can be influenced by the type of control system implemented in the organizations and by the performance it produces. The provision of new information represents an expansion of knowledge in this field.

2. Literature Review and Hypotheses of the Research

2.1. Internal control systems, interactive use of the budget and managerial performance

Internal Control Systems (ICS) and the Budgetary Control System (BCS) can have an impact on organizational and managerial performance. Simons (1995) proposes a model that provides interactive and diagnostic control systems. These two types of control are discussed with different effects in the approach to the use of control systems and the budget, as can be seen in the studies conducted by Abernethy and Brownell (1999), Bisbe and Otley (2004), Hofmann, Wald and Gleich (2012), Shen and Perera (2013), Chong and Mahama (2014), Laitinen, Lansiluoto and Salonen (2016), Neitzke and Espejo (2016). Researchers have investigated the interactive use of the budget (Abernethy & Brownell, 1999; Bisbe & Otley, 2004; Hofmann et al., 2012) and internal control systems (Hofmann et al., 2012) in organizational performance. Researchers have also investigated the interactive use of the budget (Shen & Perera, 2013; Chong & Mahama, 2014) and internal control systems (Shen & Perera, 2013; Chong & Mahama, 2014) in managerial performance.

Internal Control Systems (ICS) give motivation and direction for managers to achieve their goals, in addition to being involved in the case of major deviations, since their subordinates receive a significant level of authority and autonomy (Hofmann et al., 2012). The managers have the obligation to monitor and coordinate the implementation of the intended strategies, which can contribute to the increase of managerial performance, since the decisions taken must be aligned with the organizational objectives (Chong & Mahama, 2014). For example, companies in the textile industry that operate mainly in commodity markets can benefit from ICS, since the operating parameters are taken as predefined, with regard given to the level of strategic change, complexity of operations, and the number of subordinate decisions.

The Interactive Use of the Budget (IUB) allows managers to be regularly and personally involved in the budgeting process, as they monitor and actively intervene in the decision-making activities of their subordinates (Hofmann et al., 2012). The IUB can contribute to a meaningful increase in managerial performance due to greater flexibility regarding dialogue and respect for subordinates’ ideas. These are able to jointly discuss, challenge and debate how to organize and execute action plans in the budgets (Chong & Mahama, 2014). For example, companies in the textile industry that operate mainly in the segmented market can benefit from the IUB when there is a strategic change at a high level, as it helps to adjust the strategies in order to capitalize on the learning generated by the new ideas of their subordinates.

According to Abernethy and Brownell (1999), internal control systems are applied when there is a low level of strategic change, that is, there is little ambiguity in relation to the organizational priorities, and the nature of the work is relatively stable, has pre-established routines, and is understood for the execution of the tasks. The interactive use of the budget is applied when the level of strategic change is high, that is, there are frequent changes that demand rapid responses to new market opportunities (Abernethy & Brownell, 1999). However, the power of control systems lies not only in their distinct use, but in how much they complement each other and create a state of dynamic tension (Simons, 1995). This dynamic tension refers to two contradictory but interrelated control levers, used in a dynamic way, involving competition and complementarity (Simons, 1995).

Abernethy and Brownell (1999), extending Simons’ (1995) arguments, add that the interactive use of the BCS would be too expensive to be implemented and, therefore, the interactive use would be more appropriate in organizations with changes in the strategic typology. The authors explained that the interactive use of the budget allows the exchange of strategic information between the different levels of management. Thus, the benefits of the interactive use of the budget would outweigh the costs for carrying out the tasks. Investigating Australian public sector hospitals, the authors concluded that organizational performance is greater when managers combined high levels of strategic change and the interactive use of BCS.

Bisbe and Otley (2004) investigated whether the interactive use of the MCSs indirectly influences organizational performance through increased product innovation. In the interactive use of the MCS, they considered three control systems, budget, Balanced Scorecard and project management, but none of them had a significant relationship with product innovation, that is, they did not find an indirect relationship between the interactive use of the MCS and organizational performance. They also examined whether the interactive use of the MCS moderates the relationship between product innovation and organizational performance, and the moderation was confirmed.

Hofmann et al. (2012) analyzed the Simons’ levers (1995) in the use of the budget. First, they examined the antecedents of internal control systems and the interactive use of the budget and, then, the effects of internal control systems and the interactive use of the budget on strategy and organizational performance. They observed that the interactive use of the budget and internal control systems contribute to the formation of emerging strategies and the implementation of the intended strategies, in addition to significantly and directly influencing the organizational performance. Internal control systems had a positive impact on organizational performance, helping to achieve the organization’s goals, taking corrective actions when variations were found. The interactive use of the budget, on the other hand, had a negative impact on organizational performance, as it requires greater consumption of resources, therefore requiring more attention and more time from managers.

Shen and Perera (2013) examined the moderating effect of the strategic uncertainty on the relationship between internal control systems and the interactive use of the budget and individual performance, using the individual’s motivation as an intervening variable. They found out that internal control systems and the interactive use of the budget positively affect the individual’s motivation, which increases individual performance. When strategic uncertainty is low, that is, when the external dynamics and internal competencies of the organization are stable and predictable, managers should apply internal control systems, as individuals are motivated to carry out their activities. When strategic uncertainty is high, managers should consider the interactive use of the budget, since more interactions in such conditions are likely to motivate individuals to carry out their activities.

Chong and Mahama (2014) investigated the influence of both internal control systems and the interactive use of BCS on the managerial performance of the team, mediated by the effectiveness of the team in biotechnology companies. The assumption was that the interactive use of the budget can encourage team members to discuss and evaluate each other’s judgment, which would trigger higher quality decisions and increase the team’s managerial performance. Internal control systems, on the other hand, can provide resources and information so that the strategies and goals of the team are achieved. They found that the interactive use of BCS has a direct positive effect on the managerial performance of the team, and that this relationship is partially mediated by the effectiveness of the team. However, internal control systems did not have a significant relationship with the managerial performance of the team.

Considering the limited evidence regarding the influence of internal control systems and the budgetary control system on the performance of the individuals responsible for them, this study analyzes how internal control systems and the interactive use (Simons, 1995) of the budgetary control system can influence the managerial performance of the individuals. Thus, the first hypothesis of the research is formulated:

• H1: There is a positive relationship between internal control systems and the interactive use of the budget and managerial performance.

The non-rejection of this hypothesis will indicate that the use of internal control systems and of the budgetary control system by managers has an effect on their own performance. Thus, internal control systems and the interactive use of the budget are expected to influence the level of managerial performance.

2.2. Managerial performance and organizational commitment

Organizational commitment implies identification, involvement, and a level of emotional attachment from the individual to the organization, which is closely related to several measures of job performance (Meyer, Paunonen, Gellatly, Goffin & Jackson, 1989). The association between performance and organizational commitment is prevalent in studies using self-assessment performance measures (Meyer et al., 1989; Nouri & Parker, 1998; Fu & Deshpande, 2014; Swalhi, Zgoulli & Hofaidhllaoui, 2017).

Porter, Steers, Mowday and Boulian (1974) found out that individuals with high organizational commitment have strong belief and acceptance of the organization’s goals and values, willingness to exert a considerable effort on behalf of the organization and of remaining members of it. Individuals with a low level of organizational commitment are motivated to seek self-interest, while those with high levels are motivated to seek organizational interest (Meyer et al., 1989).

Previous research has revealed a strong link between the performance of individuals in the workplace and organizational commitment (Meyer et al., 1989; Nouri & Parker, 1998; Fu & Deshpande, 2014; Sharma & Dhar, 2016; Swalhi et al., 2017). Nouri and Parker (1998) pointed out that, besides the causal direction generally assumed in the literature, performance can lead to organizational commitment, and that the two constructs can have a reciprocal relationship. In this perspective, managers who achieve superior performance tend to have their efforts recognized by the organization, which strengthens their sense of belonging to the organization and their desire to remain in it and to achieve its established goals. Thus, the second hypothesis of the research is formulated:

• H2: There is a positive relationship between managerial performance and organizational commitment.

The non-rejection of this hypothesis will indicate that, when the managers improve the quality of their services and their effectiveness, they will identify with the organization and its goals, in addition to wishing to remain members in order to facilitate the achievement of these goals. Thus, it is expected that managerial performance will improve the managers’ organizational commitment.

2.3. Internal control systems, interactive use of the budget and organizational commitment

For Mowday, Steers and Porter (1979), organizational commitment is characterized by at least three related factors: (i) strong belief in and acceptance of the organization’s goals and values; (ii) willingness to exert considerable effort on behalf of the organization; and, (iii) a strong desire to maintain membership in the organization. In this perspective, organizational commitment is conceived not only by the expression of beliefs and opinions, but also by actions. In addition, the commitment can be perceived by the individual in different organizational dimensions.

Commitment focuses on strategy as perspective, that is, how people interpret the rights and responsibilities of the organizational world and their relationships with individuals who work around them (Simons, 2013). Commitment to others can also be identified in proactive willingness to share useful information with other people, or other units, in the organization (Simons, 2013). When defined in this fashion, commitment represents something beyond passive loyalty. It involves an active relationship such that individuals are willing to give something of themselves in order to contribute to the organization’s well being (Mowday et al., 1979).

Commitment as a construction is more global, reflecting a general affective response to the organization. It emphasizes attachment to the employing organization, including its goals and values, and tends to be more stable over time. Thus, daily events in the workplace do not lead the individual to reevaluate his attachment to the organization in general (Mowday et al., 1979). Furthermore, when the individual’s bonding behaviors or actions exceed formal and normative expectations, these are manifestations of commitment. Mowday et al. (1979) highlight that these behaviors are reflected on the organization since individuals renounce alternative courses of action and choose to connect with the organization. That is, the individual identifies himself with a particular organization and its respective objectives and wishes to remain connected to it to facilitate the achievement of these goals.

The level of commitment in organizations can be influenced by mechanisms such as internal control systems and the budgetary control system. Budget is one of the managerial control systems used by managers to coordinate and communicate strategic priorities and to induce their commitment to the organization (Abernethy & Brownell, 1999).

The results of the studies described above suggest that managerial performance in the relationship between the use of control systems (ICS and IUB) and the organizational commitment of the person responsible for meeting the budget should be analyzed both directly and through mediation. In this sense, researchers have analyzed the relationship between performance at work and organizational commitment together with organizational practices and/or psychological characteristics. Organizational commitment has been related to managerial performance in the presence of the budgetary participation (Nouri & Parker, 1998), job satisfaction (Fu & Deshpande, 2014) and organizational justice (Swalhi et al., 2017).

In this context, managerial performance can mediate the relationship between ICS and IUB and organizational commitment. This situation can be thought of based on an effect model, in which managerial performance, on one hand, is created by an antecedent variable (ICS and IUB), while, on the other, after its creation, it affects other variables which, in this study, would be organizational commitment. Based on these arguments, we formulate the third hypothesis of the research.

• H3: There is a positive relationship between internal control systems and the interactive use of the budget in organizational commitment, mediated by managerial performance.

The non-rejection of this hypothesis will indicate that the use of internal control systems and the interactive use of the BCS by the managers will only have an effect on organizational commitment if it is reflected in their managerial performance. The methodological procedures described below were used to test the hypotheses.

3. Methodological Procedures

We surveyed companies in the Brazilian textile industry in this study. The selection of this economic sector stems from the fact that it has great competitiveness in the market, which requires companies to have the capacity for innovation in their products and processes, so that they can stand out and meet the demands of the market (Rangel et al., 2010). Therefore, it is assumed that internal control systems are applied to ensure control, and the interactive use of the budget to promote organizational learning.

An invitation was sent to the managers of companies in the textile industry who were registered on LinkedIn. The terms used in the search in this network included functions of different levels of management, such as directors, managers, coordinators, supervisors, and analysts, in addition to the keyword budget. A total of 400 invitations were sent to managers, regardless of whether they work at the same company, and 250 agreed to participate. To those who accepted the invitation, the survey instrument was sent through the SurveyMonkey platform, in the period from March to April 2017, having 133 valid questionnaires answered.

The sample size was calculated using the G*Power software, which implies “evaluating the latent construct or variable that receives the greatest number of arrows or has the greatest number of predictors” (Ringle, Silva & Bido, 2014, p. 58). Three constructs were used in the study to predict organizational commitment: internal control systems, interactive use of the budget and managerial performance. Based on Ringle et al. (2014), the size of the effect used was 0.15, the significance level of α was 0.05, the power of the 1-β sample was 0.8 and three predictors. Thus, the minimum sample for the PLS-SEM model was of 77 respondents.

3.1. Constructs and research instrument

Table 1 presents the research constructs and their respective statements, in addition to the references that supported them.

The research instruments used in the research constructs were based on studies published in English. Thus, the translation of the statements to Portuguese was carried out at first and, later, the translated statements were converted back to the English language. The purpose of this process was to ensure a reliable translation of the research instruments. Before sending the questionnaire to managers, a pre-test was carried out with two research professors of graduate programs in the area of managerial accounting to analyze the translations and understand the semantic terms. When reading the research instrument, they identified assertions that were not written in an interconnectable way, so specific corrections were made.

For the research instruments of the ICS and IUB constructs (adapted from Chong & Mahama, 2014) and Organizational Commitment (Mowday et al., 1979) only adjustments to the translation were made. In the research instrument for the managerial performance construct (Denison et al., 1995), the scale was adapted, from five to seven points. Also, punctual adjustments were made to the questions to better suit the research objectives. For example, item 4 originally was ‘my overall managerial success’, and the excerpt ‘compared to other managers in the company’ was added to it.

3.2. Research instrument

The research instrument was structured in three parts. The first regards the control systems, which consist of internal control systems (ICS) and the interactive use of the budget (IUB). These constructs are based on the questionnaire prepared by Chong and Mahama (2014) and sought to verify the extent to which managers use internal control systems and the budget for issues related to ICS and IUB, where 1 = Never and 7 = Frequently.

The second part regards the manager’s managerial performance construct (mediating variable), based on the research instrument prepared by Denison et al. (1995), with performance self-assessment measures. The purpose of these questions was to assess to what extent the respondent’s managerial performance is present in the situations pointed out in the statements. Differentiated scales were used for each statement, as shown in Table 1.

The third part regards the organizational commitment (dependent variable) and used the questionnaire prepared by Mowday et al. (1979) to assess the extent to which managers show affective commitment to the organization. The purpose of these questions was to assess to what extent the situations described in the instrument characterize the organizational commitment of the respondent to where he or she works, where 1 = strongly disagree and 7 = strongly agree.

3.3. Data analysis procedures

Structural Equation Modeling (SEM) was applied to test the research hypotheses, using Partial Least Squares (Partial Least Square - PLS), through the SmartPLS statistical software. PLS-SEM is recommended to studies with small samples (Chin, 1998). The PLS-SEM analysis firstly checks the reliability of the individual indicator and the composite reliability of the model, in addition to assessing the convergent and discriminant validity of the constructs (latent variables). Then, the structural model is applied to verify the relationship between the constructs (latent variables), in order to analyze the possible relationships between an exogenous variable and an endogenous variable (Hair Jr, Hult, Ringle & Sarstedt, 2017).

4.3. Discussion of results

Hypothesis H1 predicts that there is a positive relationship between internal control systems and the interactive use of the budget and managerial performance, and, based on the results, H1 is not rejected for internal control systems (great effect, f2=0.385; β=0.527; p<0.01) and interactive use of the budget (great effect, f2=0.590; β=0.609; p<0.01). That indicates that internal control systems and the interactive use of the budget positively influence the managerial performance of the managers. Therefore, managers of companies in the textile industry that use internal control systems to achieve the objectives pre-established by upper management and use the budget interactively to stimulate the team’s organizational learning present superior managerial performance when compared to other managers.

In accordance with previous literature (Simons 1995, 2013; Hofmann et al., 2012; Chong & Mahama, 2014), it was noticed that, in the Brazilian textile industry, under the ICS approach, subordinates receive a significant level of authority and autonomy, in which the managers responsible for the controls are only involved in the decision-making process when there are significant discrepancies between the expected and actual results. Therefore, the high level of empowerment embedded in ICS is considered an important factor that contributes to improving the level of managerial performance. Likewise, the IUB approach allows the managers responsible for the budget in the textile industry to be regularly and personally involved in the activities and decisions of the subordinates. Thus, face-to-face communication between manager and subordinate, besides the flexibility and adaptation of the budget based on the IUB, helps to increase managerial performance.

The H2 hypothesis predicts that there is a positive relationship between managerial performance and organizational commitment, and the results lead to the non-rejection of the H2 both for the ICS model (medium effect, f2=0.230; β=0.486; p<0.01) and for the IUB model (medium effect, f2=0.202; β=0.489; p<0.01). Thus, it can be said that managers of companies in the textile industry who present superior managerial performance when compared to other managers are more likely to be affectionately committed to the organization. The findings support the H2, in line with the link between performance and organizational commitment reported in the studies carried out by Nouri and Parker (1998), Fu and Deshpande (2014) and Swalhi et al. (2017).

Nouri and Parker (1998) observed that organizational commitment is related to positive results for manager’s tasks, and pointed out that performance can also lead to organizational commitment. Fu and Deshpande (2014), when analyzing Chinese companies, found a positive relationship between organizational commitment and job performance. Therefore, individuals who perform better and feel a sense of shared values with the employer feel more committed to the organization.

Swalhi et al. (2017) clarified that affective commitment is closely linked to job performance in the relationship with procedural and interactional justice. Therefore, managerial performance plays an important role in organizational commitment, since managers who exert a greater effort to carry out their activities and achieve better results when compared to other managers tend to develop a stronger sense of belonging to the organization, besides its mediating role between control systems and commitment.

Hypothesis H3 postulates that there is a positive relationship between internal control systems and the interactive use of the budget and organizational commitment, mediated by managerial performance. The results support the hypothesis, since there was no significance in the direct relationship between the ICS and the IUB with organizational commitment, only in an indirect and total way, based on the mediator variable managerial performance. According to Hair Jr et al. (2017), total mediation occurs when the mediated effect is significant, not the direct effect. Thus, the mediating variable fully explains the relationship between an exogenous and an endogenous latent variable. In this context, the ICS and the IUB can only act in achieving the organizational commitment of managers of companies in the textile industry through the mediation of managerial performance. Therefore, H3 is not rejected as it has total mediation.

Internal control systems are used to establish the level of production expected by the organization as accurately as possible, in order to measure the result of managers and the team, to reward the achievement of expected results, to make adjustments and to take action if problems are identified (Shen & Perera, 2013). Thus, managers who work for companies in the textile industry that have low strategic change can benefit from the ICS to track, monitor, compare and evaluate the team in the development of the department’s activities, which leads to greater managerial performance compared to the other managers of the company. Consequently, they have a greater organizational commitment.

We observed that the ICS and the IUB are antecedent variables with an important role in creating or increasing managerial performance. Thus, the managerial performance created by the use of control systems directs to certain attitudes at work that can contribute to the increase in organizational commitment. In this study, the total mediation of managerial performance provided a more dominant contribution to organizational commitment, which reinforces the increase and development of managerial performance for the managers of these companies.

5. Conclusions

The objective of this study was to evaluate the influence of internal control systems and the interactive use of the budget on organizational commitment, mediated by managerial performance. The research results provide support for the use of Simons’ levers (1995), regarding the relevance of internal control systems and/or the interactive use of the budget as a stimulus to the managers’ managerial performance, thus corroborating with the H1. These two systems are relevant to model the behavior of managers towards performing their tasks in line with the organization’s goals.

The results indicate that managerial performance positively affects organizational commitment, which allows the non-rejection of the H2. Managers who improve their performance identify with the organization and its goals, in such a way that they develop a sense of loyalty and a desire to remain in the organization, in line with Nouri and Parker (1998), Fu and Deshpande (2014) and Swalhi et al. (2017).

The study also confirmed that internal control systems and the interactive use of the budget positively affect organizational commitment, and that managerial performance eases the relationship between them, leading to the non-rejection of the H3. Internal control systems or the interactive use of the budget encourage managers to do their best in their tasks in the organization, increasing managerial performance (mediating variable), in addition to inspiring loyalty, the desire to stay and the efforts of managers towards the goals and values of the organization, increasing organizational commitment.

Based on the results, it is concluded that internal control systems and the budgetary control system influence the organizational commitment, through the mediation of managerial performance, in companies in the textile industry that were surveyed. It is noteworthy that internal control systems and/or the interactive use of the budget influence managerial performance, just as performance influences organizational commitment, in addition to being a mediating variable between the control systems and organizational commitment.

The study contributes to the literature by jointly addressing internal control systems and the use of the budget, based on two of Simons’ levers (1995) (diagnostic control systems and interactive control systems), managerial performance and organizational commitment in Brazilian companies in the textile industry. It also contributes to the daily lives of these organizations, since stimulating managerial performance can foster greater organizational commitment of managers, mainly by fully mediating the relationship between the ICS and the IUB with organizational commitment. Despite the study’s contributions to the researched scope, they are subject to limitations.

As limitations of this study, the research data represent the respondents’ perceptions and it is possible that they correspond to the desired practices, not those effectively performed in the companies where they work. Another limitation is that the level of strategic change and the type of market that companies in the textile industry seek to serve were not identified, and this information could be antecedent variables of how the company uses internal control systems and the budget. The research sample is made up of companies in the textile industry and, certainly, this sample is not representative of the other segments, limiting the generalization of the research results.

It is recommended for future studies to investigate the relationship between internal control systems and the interactive use of the budget and other types of behaviors, such as organizational citizenship and altruism, and also, to consider the level of strategic change or strategic uncertainty as an antecedent of internal control systems and the interactive use of the budget. Other mediation variables can be used, such as job satisfaction, creativity, motivational factors, cognitive factors and role autonomy. It is also recommended to adopt the other levers of Simons (1995), the belief system and the boundary system to verify how controls and the budget are designed within organizations.
